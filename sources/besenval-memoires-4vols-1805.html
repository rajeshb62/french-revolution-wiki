Pierre-Victor, baron de Besenval (1721–1791), Mémoires de M. le baron de Besenval, lieutenant-général des armées du roi, sous Louis XV et Louis XVI, grand’croix de l’ordre de Saint-Louis, gouverneur des ville et château de Haguenau, 4 vols. Paris: F. Buisson (posthumous, c. 1805). Extracted raw text from University of California (UCAL) digitised copies. Vol. 1 (Tome I, UCAL b3343817) approximately 402 pages, 374 KB; Vol. 2 (Tome II, UCAL b3343818) approximately 388 pages, 370 KB; Vol. 3 (Tome III, UCAL b3343820) approximately 452 pages, 434 KB; Vol. 4 (Tome IV, UCAL b3343819) approximately 436 pages, 420 KB. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol1.txt]

Besenval was a Swiss-born soldier and courtier who served as Lieutenant-General of the royal armies under Louis XV and Louis XVI. Born in Soleure to a patrician family of Savoyard origin, he entered the régiment des Gardes as a cadet at age nine, served in the campaigns of 1735 and 1748 (Bohemia), and rose to become a key figure at the French court, deeply involved in military administration and court politics. He was also the author of various literary works and a member of the literary academy formed by officers at Drévenich during the 1757 campaign. His memoirs were published posthumously under the editorship of his testamentary executor, A. J. de Ségur. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol1.txt]

The memoirs cover the period from approximately 1735 through July 1789 — Besenval died in 1791, so the narrative breaks off at the outset of the Revolution, but his account of the July 1789 crisis (as the commander responsible for the troops around Paris) is of exceptional value. The work is structured not as a continuous chronological narrative but as a series of separate articles or mémoires on specific subjects, persons, and events — a format typical of the genre. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol1.txt]

Volume Structure

Volume 1 (Tome I) — Switzerland, Prussia, the Seven Years’ War, Court Life under Louis XV

The first volume opens with A. J. de Ségur’s Avertissement (as executor of Besenval’s will) and a Notice sur M. le baron de Besenval providing biographical context, then proceeds to the memoir articles:

  1. Des Suisses, et du changement de leur constitution militaire en France, en 1764 — Besenval, himself Swiss-born and a colonel of the Swiss Guards, writes on the Swiss military constitution in France and the reforms of 1764. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol1.txt]

  2. Anecdote singulière de la Suisse — A remarkable anecdote from Switzerland. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol1.txt]

  3. Du Roi de Prusse — Observations on Frederick the Great, his character and military methods. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol1.txt]

  4. Anecdote du Roi de Prusse — Particular anecdote concerning Frederick. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol1.txt]

  5. Anecdote sur la Campagne de 1744 — Besenval’s own experience in the campaign of 1744 during the War of the Austrian Succession. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol1.txt]

  6. Bataille d’Hastenbeck, et ce qui l’a précédée; Réflexions sur la conduite de M. de Maillebois; Caractères de quelques Généraux — The Battle of Hastenbeck (26 July 1757) and the campaign of 1757 in Germany during the Seven Years’ War, with character sketches of the French generals. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol1.txt]

  7. Bataille de Fillinghauzen; ce qui l’a précédée; Caractères de quelques Généraux — The Battle of Villinghausen (15–16 July 1761) and preceding events, with further general character sketches. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol1.txt]

Further articles cover court anecdotes, the comte de Frise, Montesquieu at Venice, the maréchal de Richelieu, the Jansenist-Jesuit controversies, and:

  1. Principes de la haine des Jésuites contre M. de Choiseul — The Jesuits’ hostility to the duc de Choiseul, leading up to the expulsion of the Society from France. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol1.txt]

Volume 2 (Tome II) — Political Intrigues, the Choiseul Disgrace, Louis XV’s Death, the Early Reign of Louis XVI

The second volume is entirely concerned with court politics and ministerial intrigues:

  1. Intrigue de M. de Lavaugayon contre M. le duc de Choiseul, en 1760 — The earliest of the cabals against Choiseul, led by Lavaugayon. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol2.txt]

  2. Dernière intrigue qui détermina la disgrâce du duc de Choiseul; détails sur l’abbé de la Ville — The final intrigue that brought down Choiseul in 1770, with particulars on the abbé de La Ville. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol2.txt]

  3. Comment le duc de Choiseul perdit la charge de colonel-général des Suisses, et les suites de cet événement — Choiseul’s loss of the colonel-generalcy of the Swiss regiments — a matter directly affecting Besenval’s own military career. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol2.txt]

  4. Anecdotes sur le renvoi des Jésuites — The expulsion of the Jesuits from France (1762–1764). ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol2.txt]

  5. Mort du Roi Louis XV — The death of Louis XV (10 May 1774), including details of the smallpox that killed him and the court’s reaction. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol2.txt]

  6. Exil du duc d’Aiguillon — The exile of the duc d’Aiguillon, Louis XV’s last foreign minister, following the accession of Louis XVI. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol2.txt]

  7. Anecdote du chancelier de Maupeou, et du président de Lamoignon — The chancelier Maupeou’s coup against the parlements (1771) and the role of président de Lamoignon. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol2.txt]

  8. Caractère du chancelier — A character portrait of Maupeou. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol2.txt]

  9. Des Parlemens — Besenval’s reflections on the parlements as institutions. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol2.txt]

  10. Anecdote de Louis XV; réflexions sur M. Necker — A Louis XV anecdote and reflexions on Necker, his character and financial administration. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol2.txt]

  11. Origine et fortune de M. de Vergennes, Ministre — The rise of the comte de Vergennes, Louis XVI’s foreign minister. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol2.txt]

  12. Mort du chevalier de Muy, ministre de la Guerre; nomination de M. de Saint-Germain à sa place; d’autres Événemens; Caractères de quelques Gens de la Cour — The death of the chevalier de Muy, Saint-Germain’s appointment as Minister of War, and sketches of various court figures. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol2.txt]

  13. Comment madame de Guémené perdit la place de Gouvernante des Enfans de France, et comment la duchesse de Polignac lui succéda — The change of gouvernante of the Children of France, from the princesse de Guémené to the duchesse de Polignac — a key event in court politics under Louis XVI. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol2.txt]

  14. Combat de M. le comte d’Artois et de M. le duc de Bourbon — The duel between the comte d’Artois (the future Charles X) and the duc de Bourbon, a celebrated court affair. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol2.txt]

  15. Comment M. de Castries et M. de Ségur sont parvenus au Ministère — How the maréchal de Castries (Marine) and the maréchal de Ségur (Guerre) attained their ministerial positions — Besenval was intimately involved in the latter’s appointment. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol2.txt]

Volume 3 (Tome III) — Justice Reform, the Parlement Crisis, Cardinal Rohan, the Calonne Ministry, the Assembly of Notables, the Pre-Revolution, and the July Days of 1789

This is the most important volume for the French Revolution wiki. It covers the political crisis of 1786–1789, culminating in Besenval’s personal role as commander of the troops around Paris during the July 1789 uprising.

  1. De M. de Lamoignon, président à mortier au Parlement de Paris; et de la réforme des abus de la Justice dans ce Parlement — Besenval’s extensive account of his friend Lamoignon, his project for judicial reform, and the struggle between the crown and the parlements. Includes the Mémoire sur la Réforme de la Justice and its subsidiary proposals: reform of judicial instruction, reform of judges’ remuneration, reform of clerks, reform of the greffes, and the King’s formal response. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol3.txt]

  2. Comité de Finances — The financial committee and the state of the royal treasury. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol3.txt]

  3. De la Société des Rois — Reflections on the nature of monarchy and princely society. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol3.txt]

  4. Procès du Cardinal de Rohan — The trial of Cardinal de Rohan (1785–1786) for his role in the Diamond Necklace affair. Besenval, a well-placed courtier, provides inside details on this scandal that fatally weakened the monarchy’s prestige. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol3.txt]

  5. Lettre du baron de Besenval au maréchal de Ségur, ministre de la Guerre, en date du 6 novembre 1786 — Official correspondence on military matters. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol3.txt]

  6. Lettre du baron de Besenval au comte de Ségur, ministre plénipotentiaire du Roi auprès de l’Impératrice de Russie, en date du 6 mars 1787. Assemblée des Notables. Comment M. de Lamoignon est parvenu à être garde-des-sceaux, et M. de Brienne, archevêque de Toulouse, à la tête des finances — A crucial section covering the Assembly of Notables (1787), Lamoignon’s appointment as Keeper of the Seals, and Brienne’s rise to head the finances. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol3.txt]

  7. Réflexions sur le Plan de M. de Calonne, et sur son exécution — Besenval’s critique of Calonne’s financial plan presented to the Assembly of Notables. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol3.txt]

  8. La Hollande abandonnée. Le comte de Brienne, ministre de la Guerre, et M. de la Luzerne, de la Marine. Conseil de guerre établi. La guerre évitée. — The Dutch crisis of 1787, the Patriot uprising, Prussian intervention, and France’s failure to intervene due to financial exhaustion. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol3.txt]

  9. Édit d’un emprunt, et d’un autre pour accorder le droit de Citoyen aux Protestans, portés par le Roi au Parlement. Celui de l’emprunt enregistré par ordre exprès — The struggle over the edict for a new loan and the edict granting civil rights to Protestants (the Edict of Tolerance, 1787–1788). ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol3.txt]

  10. Portrait de M. le duc d’Orléans. Exil de ce prince et des conseillers Freteau et l’abbé Sabatier de Cabre — A character portrait of the duc d’Orléans (Philippe-Égalité) and the exile of the duc and two conseillers following their protests during the séance royale. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol3.txt]

  11. L’archevêque de Toulouse troque son archevêché contre celui de Sens — Brienne’s exchange of the archbishopric of Toulouse for that of Sens. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol3.txt]

  12. Further sections on the Brienne ministry, the May Edicts (8 May 1788), the abolition of the parlements, the establishment of the Cour plénière, provincial resistance in Brittany and Dauphiné, the financial collapse, Brienne’s resignation, the recall of Necker, and the second Assembly of Notables (November 1788). ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol3.txt]

  13. Complot dirigé par Laclos et le comte de Mirabeau, en faveur du duc d’Orléans — The conspiracy of Laclos (author of Les Liaisons dangereuses) and Mirabeau on behalf of the duc d’Orléans during the pre-revolutionary crisis. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol3.txt]

  14. Ce qui m’est arrivé à la Révolution de 1789 — Besenval’s personal account of the revolutionary crisis. As lieutenant-general commanding the troops concentrated around Paris (the “camp of the Champ de Mars” and the troops at Saint-Denis, the Bastille, etc.), he was at the centre of the military response to the July 1789 uprising. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol3.txt]

  15. 12, 13 et 14 juillet 1789. Ma prison et mon Procès — The most important section for the wiki. Besenval’s detailed day-by-day account of the July Days — the dismissal of Necker, the military preparations, the fall of the Bastille, and his own arrest and prosecution. He was made a scapegoat for the government’s failure to suppress the insurrection, arrested at Provins while attempting to flee, and brought back to Paris for trial before the Châtelet. His account of his imprisonment in Brie-Comte-Robert and his trial is a unique source on the immediate aftermath of 14 July from the perspective of the defeated royal command. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol3.txt]

Volume 4 (Tome IV) — Mélanges Littéraires et Philosophiques

The fourth volume is entirely different in character — it contains Besenval’s literary and philosophical works, not his memoirs proper. The editor’s Avertissement explains that the volume originated in a literary academy formed by officers at Drévenich during the 1757 campaign, where each member contributed prose or verse. The volume opens with a letter from Crébillon fils reviewing Besenval’s manuscript. Contents:

  1. Le Spleen — A prose piece on melancholy. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol4.txt]
  2. Idées politiques et militaires — Political and military reflections. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol4.txt]
  3. Les Amans soldats — A tale of soldier-lovers. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol4.txt]
  4. Féerie — A fairy tale. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol4.txt]
  5. Socrate et Gassendi — A philosophical dialogue. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol4.txt]
  6. Alonzo — A story. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol4.txt]
  7. Cœlia — A story. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol4.txt]
  8. Réflexions sur la Comédie — Reflections on comedy. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol4.txt]
  9. L’Hermite — The Hermit, a tale. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol4.txt]
  10. Réflexions sur le Ton — Reflections on tone in society. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol4.txt]
  11. Histoire des Revenans — A ghost story. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol4.txt]
  12. Pensées détachées — Detached thoughts and maxims. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol4.txt]
  13. Nouvelle espagnole — A Spanish novella. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol4.txt]
  14. Du Chagrin — On sorrow. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol4.txt]
  15. Anecdote bretonne — A Breton anecdote. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol4.txt]
  16. Première Scène d’une Comédie — First scene of a comedy. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol4.txt]
  17. Aventure et Conversation de M. le Baron de Besenval avec une dame de Wesel — Besenval’s own encounter and conversation with a lady of Wesel. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol4.txt]
  18. Opinion des Turcs sur les femmes — Turkish opinions on women. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol4.txt]
  19. Disgrâce de madame des Ursins, et ce qui l’a occasionnée — Historical account of the disgrace of the princesse des Ursins (18th century Spanish court). ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol4.txt]
  20. De la Douleur — On pain, physical and moral. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol4.txt]
  21. Traduction d’un ouvrage chinois sur les jardins — Translation of a Chinese work on gardens. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol4.txt]
  22. Épître à Damon — Verse epistle. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol4.txt]
  23. Épître au comte de F* — Verse epistle. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol4.txt]
  24. À l’abbé Allaire, mon précepteur, en lui envoyant une collection d’auteurs latins — Verse to his former tutor. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol4.txt]
  25. Sur la mort du comte de Frise, neveu du maréchal de Saxe — On the death of the comte de Frise. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol4.txt]

Revolution-Related Coverage Guide

Volume 3 is the principal revolution source. The following sections are of particular interest:

  • Procès du Cardinal de Rohan (Tome III, art. 4) — The Diamond Necklace affair, which fatally undermined the moral authority of the monarchy and is widely considered a proximate cause of the pre-revolutionary crisis. Besenval’s court connections give him access to details unavailable to the press. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol3.txt]

  • Lettres to Ségur; Assemblée des Notables; Calonne’s Plan (Tome III, arts. 5–7) — Besenval’s correspondence provides documentary evidence of the military and financial situation in 1786–87, and his critique of Calonne reflects the views of the court military establishment. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol3.txt]

  • The Brienne Ministry and the Parlement Crisis (Tome III, arts. 8–12) — The entire pre-revolutionary crisis of 1787–88, including the exile of the Parlement, the May Edicts, the Cour plénière, the provincial revolts, and the collapse of the Brienne ministry. Besenval was a close friend of Lamoignon and has unique insight into the government’s internal deliberations. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol3.txt]

  • Portrait of the duc d’Orléans (Tome III, art. 10) — Besenval’s unflattering character sketch of Philippe-Égalité, written by a military courtier who despised Orléans’s ambitions. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol3.txt]

  • The Laclos-Mirabeau-Orléans Conspiracy (Tome III, art. 13) — Besenval’s account of the plot to place the duc d’Orléans on the throne or in the regency, a conspiracy theory widely held in royalist and moderate circles but documented here by a participant-observer. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol3.txt]

  • The July Days: Besenval’s Command, Arrest, and Trial (Tome III, arts. 14–15) — These are the most valuable pages in the entire work for the wiki. Besenval was the officer commanding the royal troops stationed around Paris. His account of 12–14 July 1789 — the decision not to use force against the Parisian insurrection, his loss of control over the troops, his flight, and his arrest — is the senior royal commander’s own version of events. The trial narrative also contains testimony from witnesses and documents that survive nowhere else. This is a rare source from the defeated royalist military side, complementing the popular/national assembly narratives that dominate other sources. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol3.txt]

Quality

OCRed text from University of California library scans. The French text preserves late 18th-century orthography (e.g., “événemens,” “foible,” “paroît”). The OCR quality is variable — some pages have significant scan artifacts (especially in Tome IV, where the Greek and decorative typefaces cause frequent misreads). The text is serviceable for keyword search but requires care for verbatim quotation. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol1.txt]

Value to the Wiki

Besenval’s memoirs are valuable as:

  1. A court insider’s view of the Louis XV and Louis XVI administrations — As a colonel of the Swiss Guards, lieutenant-general, and frequent at Versailles, Besenval had direct access to ministers (Choiseul, Maupeou, Ségur, Castries, Lamoignon), to court factions (the Choiseul circle, the Polignac set, the Maurepas party), and to the royal family itself. No other source in the wiki covers the court politics of 1760–1789 from this angle in such detail. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol2.txt]

  2. A military commander’s account of the July 1789 crisis — Besenval was the man on the ground, responsible for the very troops that were supposed to suppress the Paris insurrection. His account of 12–14 July is the closest thing we have to the official royal military report on the fall of the Bastille, written by the commander who failed to hold Paris. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol3.txt]

  3. A unique perspective on the Parlement-Monarchy struggle — As Lamoignon’s intimate friend, Besenval documents the internal debates of the government during the 1787–88 crisis from a position of trust. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol3.txt]

  4. A Swiss officer’s perspective on French institutions — Besenval’s Swiss origins and his role as colonel of the Swiss Guards give him a distinctive angle on the French military constitution, the Swiss regiments, and the relationship between foreign troops and the French crown. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol1.txt]

  5. Literary mélanges (Volume 4) — While less directly useful for the revolution wiki, Tome IV shows Besenval as a man of letters and provides the philosophical and literary context for his memoir judgments. It also includes Crébillon fils’s editorial criticism of Besenval’s writing style, which sheds light on the literary culture of the 18th-century military aristocracy. ^[source: raw/besenval-memoires-vol4.txt]
